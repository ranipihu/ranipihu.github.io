Auditory Threshold Test

Find the highest pure tone you can hear, measured in 50 Hz steps from 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

How to test

  1. Set your computer’s system volume to 50% and keep it at that level for the entire test. Use familiar headphones or speakers in a quiet room.
  2. Play a tone, then raise the frequency by 50 Hz until it is no longer audible.
  3. Go back down in 50 Hz steps to confirm the highest tone you can hear, then record it.

What the result means for design

  • Auditory displays: keep important cues well below a listener’s upper limit; mid-frequency cues are more reliably heard across users.
  • Alarms: do not rely on a single very high-pitched tone. Use a lower-frequency component, repetition, and a visual or vibration backup.
  • Device feedback: offer volume and sound choices; short, distinct tones are easier to notice than faint or high-frequency beeps.
  • Limits: age-related high-frequency hearing loss, headphone/speaker response, room noise, browser/device processing, and volume all affect this informal result.

Safety: this is an educational screening activity, not a hearing test. Stop if a tone is uncomfortable or causes ringing; do not increase volume to chase a result.
