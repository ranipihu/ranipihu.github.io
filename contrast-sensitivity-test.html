Contrast Sensitivity Lab

A classroom demonstration inspired by Pelli–Robson letter charts. It is not a clinical vision test.

Before you begin

  1. Place the computer so the screen is at eye level, about an arm’s length away.
  2. Set screen brightness to a medium level and complete the test in a medium-lit room.
  3. Disable night/blue-light filters if possible and use your usual visual correction.
  4. Identify the letter using the on-screen keys. Guess if unsure; do not use image zoom or screenshots.

Method: Three letters appear at each contrast and the test descends continuously, including levels below 1.5%. It ends only after four incorrect responses in a row. At the 0.05% floor, the near-invisible level repeats until that stopping rule is met. The estimated threshold is the lowest contrast correctly identified before the error run.

Assignment discussion points

  • Legibility: Text, icons, and critical controls must stay above the user’s usable contrast threshold, with an allowance for glare, small type, fatigue, and viewing distance.
  • Visual ergonomics: Ambient light, screen angle, brightness, and reflections can raise thresholds. Repeat the test under controlled and low-light conditions to demonstrate this effect.
  • Accessibility: WCAG contrast ratios are design safeguards, not individual clinical measurements. Design for the lower-performing or more fatigued viewer rather than a single measured threshold.
  • Low-contrast interfaces: Placeholder text, disabled states, thin fonts, and gray-on-gray controls can become functionally invisible even when a person can still read high-contrast text.
