How small a silence can you hear?

Three equal white-noise bursts are presented on each trial. One has a silent gap exactly in its center. Find it; the gap adapts to your responses.

Listening station
Set up before you listen

For a reliable, comfortable result, take a minute to prepare:

  • Use headphones in a quiet room. Do not take the test while driving, walking near traffic, or operating equipment.
  • Set a low, comfortable volume. The noise should be easy to hear but never loud, sharp, or uncomfortable. Do not increase volume to find the gap.
  • Pause if needed. Stop immediately if you feel discomfort, ringing, pain, dizziness, or fatigue.
  • Listen without rushing. Each option can be replayed before you choose; pick the one with a brief silence in its middle.

How it works

  1. Each burst is continuous white noise, except one center gap.
  2. Two correct choices shorten the next gap; one error lengthens it.
  3. The step size decreases from 16 to 8 to 4 ms as reversals accumulate.
  4. After six reversals, the final threshold is the average of the last four reversal points.

Why temporal resolution matters

Temporal resolution is the auditory system’s ability to separate events that happen close together. A short detectable gap signals how precisely a listener can track rapid changes in sound—not simply how loudly they hear.

Speech perceptionFast consonant transitions, stop releases, and brief pauses carry intelligibility—especially in noise or rapid speech.
AlertsSeparating urgent tones in time makes alarm sequences less likely to blur into one undifferentiated sound.
Auditory interfacesDistinct onset timing and adequate spacing help users identify confirmation sounds, messages, and competing cues.
Time-critical feedbackFeedback must arrive promptly and remain temporally distinct so a person can link it to an action and react safely.
