How small can a direction become?

A calibrated Tumbling E–inspired task. Keep a fixed viewing distance, identify the opening, and let the staircase find your smallest reliable feature.

Before you begin · accuracy checklist

  1. Set browser zoom to 100% and use the display’s native resolution. Enter the screen’s physical diagonal and native pixel dimensions below; this is what converts pixels into millimetres.
  2. Measure the viewing distance from your eyes to the screen (not from the chair), enter it, and keep your head at that distance throughout the task. A chair back or a small tape mark on the desk helps.
  3. Use the correction you normally wear for screen viewing. Keep room lighting even, clean the screen, and avoid reflections, glare, dim mode, or a brightness setting that makes black look gray.
  4. Test in a relaxed state and keep both eyes open for a binocular estimate. For an eye-by-eye comparison, cover the other eye with an opaque card—do not press on it—and run a separate task for each eye.
  5. Choose the direction of the opening promptly, even when unsure. Do not lean forward, squint, or move closer; the task is designed to find the point where recognition is no longer reliable.

Important: This is an educational screen task, not a clinical eye examination. Results are approximate and depend on accurate display specifications.

What this means for display design

Design takeaway: Text and symbols must be comfortably larger than a threshold feature because reading needs fast, repeated recognition—not just a correct forced choice. Safety warnings should add generous size, contrast, and redundancy; at longer distances their physical size must grow in direct proportion to distance to preserve the same visual angle.
